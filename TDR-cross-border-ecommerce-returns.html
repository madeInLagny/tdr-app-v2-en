Cross-border eCommerce returns

Cross-Border eCommerce Returns
Without Paying Duty Twice

Every cross-border return carries a customs cost. Recover the duty you already paid and avoid paying it again.

Returns should not be a customs cost you pay twice.

When an international customer returns an order, the duty paid on the way in is lost unless you claim it back, and goods coming back can be charged again. Duty drawback and Returned Goods Relief recover or avoid that cost in the US, EU and UK.

The return crosses the border

Customer returns, exchanges and rejected orders move goods back across customs borders, with duty questions at every crossing.

Duty is paid, then lost

Duty paid when the order was imported is not refunded automatically when the goods go back.

Two routes recover it

Duty drawback refunds duty on imported goods that leave again. Returned Goods Relief avoids duty on goods that were exported and come back.

Where cross-border returns cost you money

A cross-border return is not only a shipping cost. It is a chain of customs events, and each one can add cost if it is not managed.

The most common leaks

  • Duty paid on the original import is not refunded when the customer sends the goods back, unless you claim it.
  • Goods returned to the market they were exported from can be charged duty again if relief is not claimed at re-import.
  • Returned items cannot be matched to their original shipment, so claims lack evidence.
  • Time limits are missed: drawback and Returned Goods Relief each have deadlines that run from import or export.
  • Worn or tried-on items come back and cannot qualify for Returned Goods Relief.
What recovery can be worth
Returns can generate 6-8% cost savings on original import duties. In the US, drawback can recover up to 99% of duties paid. In the EU and the UK, 100% of duties and VAT can be recovered.

Returns in apparel, sporting goods and luxury

The same customs rules apply to every product, but the way returns behave differs by sector.

What to watch in your sector

  • Apparel and sporting goods: high return volumes make item-level records essential. Worn or tried-on items are not eligible for Returned Goods Relief.
  • Luxury goods: higher unit values mean each return carries more duty, so each unmatched return costs more.
  • Multiple warehouses and carriers: returns pass through several hands, and the records need to be joined before a claim.
  • Marketplaces and 3PLs: the party importing the returned goods may not be the party that exported them, which affects VAT relief in the UK and in the EU.
  • Exchanges and partial returns: only the returned items can be claimed, so claims need to be item-level.

Which route applies in the US, EU and UK?

The route depends on how the goods moved. Duty drawback applies to goods imported and later sent back abroad. Returned Goods Relief applies to goods that were exported and come back.
