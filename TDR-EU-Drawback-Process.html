EU duty drawback for retailers

How TDR EU duty drawback works for returned orders

EU duty drawback lets DTC retailers reclaim import duties paid on goods that are returned by customers, refunded, and re-exported.

Check your eligibility

A quick check

Who Qualifies?

Your business may qualify when these conditions are met.

EU-registered importer

You sell direct to consumers from outside the EU.

Orders above EUR 150

The order value is above the refund threshold.

Import at market value

The customs value matches the item's retail value.

Unused and refunded

The customer returns the unused item and receives a refund.

Re-exported in 3 months

The returned goods leave the EU within three months of import.

From import to refund

The end-to-end flow

Every eligible order is coordinated from the initial shipment through the final customs refund.

  1. 1 Retailer

    Ships goods and sends manifest

    The retailer ships the goods and shares the order manifest with the carrier and TDR.

  2. 2 TDR

    Pre-clearance and duty deferment

    TDR performs pre-clearance, instructs the carrier, and duties are paid from the retailer's deferment account.

  3. 3 Carrier

    Delivers goods into the EU

    The carrier brings the goods to the EU and forwards cleared orders to consignees.

  4. 5 Carrier

    Consolidates returned goods

    The carrier brings all returns to a consolidation center located in the EU.

  5. 6 TDR or external broker

    Clears bulk return shipments

    TDR or an external broker clears the returned goods out of the EU in bulk shipments.

  6. 7 TDR

    Reconciles data and submits the claim

    TDR reconciles import and export data at item level and submits the refund request to EU Customs.

  7. 8 EU Customs

    Approves and credits the refund

    After regulatory checks, Customs approves the refund and credits the retailer's deferment account.

Your part is clear

Your role as a retailer

You stay in control of your customer and sales experience. TDR manages the customs work around it.

  • You are the importer of record for goods entering Europe.
  • You provide a manifest of imported orders under EU customs rules.
  • Manifest values match each item's retail market value.
  • You use an integrated carrier, or share carrier details for validation.
  • TDR handles import clearance; duties use your deferment account.
  • Only unused, refunded, and timely re-exported items qualify.
  • You use an integrated export broker, or share broker details for validation.
